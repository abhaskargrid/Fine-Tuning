1. Objective and Problem Statement
The objective of this analysis is to implement and evaluate a Best-of-N sampling strategy for inference-time compute scaling using a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B model. By generating $N \in [5, 10, 20, 50]$ candidates per problem and selecting the best outcome via an isolated sandbox, this study aims to quantify sample efficiency, profile inference latency, establish an optimal $N$ for macOS hardware, and compare the resource trade-offs between inference-time search (Best-of-N) and train-time optimization (Reinforcement Learning).
2. Methodology & Implementation
A Best-of-N generation pipeline was implemented using the Hugging Face transformers library, generating candidate solutions with a temperature of 0.6 to encourage diversity. To optimize execution latency, an "early stopping" mechanism was integrated: candidates were evaluated sequentially in the isolated sandbox, and the loop was immediately halted (break) upon the first successful test case execution.
To ensure the model's generalized reasoning capabilities were tested, evaluations were conducted on two completely unseen, held-out slices of the HumanEval dataset:
Trial 1: 20 unseen problems (test[60:80])
Trial 2: 50 unseen problems (test[80:130])

Anomaly Detection (The Early Stopping Advantage):
In both Pareto curves, a distinct anomaly occurs: $N=20$ executes faster than $N=10$.
In Trial 1: $N=10$ took 254s, while $N=20$ took only 241s.
In Trial 2: $N=10$ took 906s, while $N=20$ took only 721s.
This proves the efficacy of the early stopping mechanism. At $N=20$, the increased candidate pool statistically guarantees a correct answer earlier in the sequence, allowing the evaluation loop to halt prematurely and save massive amounts of compute time.
Optimal N for Mac:
The optimal parameter is definitively $N=20$. It maximizes accuracy (80% and 70% respectively) while maintaining a highly efficient wall-clock time due to the early stopping phenomenon. Scaling to $N=50$ offers diminishing returns, yielding minimal accuracy gains while doubling the execution time (up to ~30 minutes) due to GPU compute saturation and thermal throttling.
6. Comparison: RL vs. Best-of-N
This data directly quantifies the trade-offs between Train-Time Compute (RL) and Inference-Time Compute (Best-of-N):
VRAM Usage: The profiling data explicitly proves that Best-of-N is highly VRAM-efficient. Across all scales ($N=5$ to $N=50$), peak memory allocation remained entirely flat at ~2951 MB.
Wall-Clock Time: To achieve peak accuracy via Best-of-N, the system pays a heavy penalty in inference latency, requiring up to 30 minutes to search for solutions.
Reinforcement Learning (RL): In contrast, RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) learn from rollouts during training. This requires massive VRAM overhead (updating policy and reference models) and significant wall-clock time during the training phase. However, post-training, an RL-optimized model can output the correct solution at $N=1$, reducing inference latency to seconds.
7. Final Benefit & Conclusion
Best-of-N sampling effectively trades wall-clock time for VRAM efficiency, making it an ideal search strategy for resource-constrained hardware like Mac laptops where VRAM is limited but temporary compute time is available. The analysis successfully identifies $N=20$ as the optimal configuration to balance high coding accuracy with sustainable system resource utilization.
